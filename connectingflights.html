UX Research Project with CATR, NRC

Connecting   Flights

Understanding Passenger Rights and Flight Disruption Experiences.

UX Research Lead & Design Lead  ·  8 Months  ·  Team of 5

Air travel comes with rights that most passengers do not fully understand, including those under the Air Passenger Protection Regulations, or APPR. Project Inform Me Right raises awareness about these regulations through a website that includes a compensation eligibility checker and a more accessible version of the APPR. This helps passengers understand their rights and check if they qualify for compensation.

02

What are the factors contributing to the communication gap between the passengers’ experiences and their expectations during a flight disruption?

The Challenge

Passengers often only learn about their compensation rights after a delay or missed connection has already happened. Airlines already offer quality service and compensation exists for eligible passengers, but this information rarely reaches them when they need it.

The objective was to identify why passengers lack clear information about their rights, understand how past experiences shape their expectations, and assess how their actual experience compares during a flight disruption.

The focus

Uncover the barriers, expectations, and policy gaps behind why passengers stay uninformed about their compensation rights.

The Gap

Passengers already have access to awareness, communication, service, and compensation, but no one connects the dots for them when it matters most.

03

Understanding the System

There is already a system in place that works well under normal conditions. Airlines, airports, and regulatory boards each play their part in keeping passengers informed and served.

But when a disruption occurs, two things fail. Passengers do not receive clear information about their rights, and they do not receive the level of service they were promised. This research looks closely at that failure.

Research Approach

The research followed a mixed methods approach. It began with secondary research into the APPR and existing airline communication practices, which shaped a quantitative survey and a set of semi-structured qualitative interviews with passengers and subject matter experts.

A final round of validation interviews tested the concept directly with people who had recently faced a flight disruption, to see if it actually addressed what they had described.

Step 01: Secondary Research

Reviewed the APPR and existing literature on flight delays, missed connections, and airline policies before speaking with anyone.

Step 02: Quantitative Research

A survey answered by 107 respondents, focused on international travelers aged 18 to 30, flying economy, with at least one connecting flight.

Step 03: Qualitative Research

In depth interviews with 12 participants to understand their real experiences, and 4 Subject Matter Experts (SMEs) to understand industry perspectives.

Step 04: Validation Interviews

3 follow up interviews with participants who had recently faced a disruption, used to test and refine the findings.

05

Key Insights

Insight 01

Insufficient communication breaks trust

Passengers lose trust in airlines due to poor communication, since airlines cannot always disclose disruption details.

Insight 02

Passengers don't know their rights

Passengers are unaware of their rights because there are no proactive measures from airlines to educate them.

Insight 03

The APPR document is hard to use

Passengers struggle to find and understand the regulations document because it holds so much information.

Insight 04

Eligibility for compensation is unclear

Passengers are unsure about their eligibility for compensation because it's difficult to find and understand.

The APPR already protects passengers during a disruption.
Most passengers never get to feel that protection.

Every insight pointed back to the same root problem: passengers cannot access rights that already exist. The focus became creating awareness, simplifying the APPR, and helping passengers understand their eligibility, which is exactly what the research kept asking for.

08

Project Inform-Me-Right

Create awareness about the APPR, and create a website with a compensation eligibility checker and a more accessible APPR.

1. Awareness

Posters and digital ads about the Inform-Me-Right website can be displayed at the boarding gates and waiting lounges where passengers naturally spend time. Brochures about the website can be included in flight seat pockets and handed out at airline counters, and the ads appear on in-flight entertainment systems.

Staff at check in counters and customer service desks are trained to mention the website when a passenger runs into a disruption. The same message shows up earlier too, during ticket booking, in social media posts, and on animated screens around the airport, so a passenger sees it more than once before they actually need it.

2. Eligibility Checker

Passengers enter a few basic details about their flight, including the departure and arrival airports, the date, and the reason. The checker weighs this against the rules in the APPR to work out whether the airline is responsible for the disruption.

If the passenger qualifies, the site lays out the steps to file a claim along with the exact section of the APPR that applies. If they do not qualify, it explains why in plain language and points to the rule behind that decision, so the outcome makes sense.

Navigation stays intuitive and clear, directing users to relevant pages with minimal clicks, using concise plain language, and organizing content into short, distinct sections.

Expected Impact

Project Inform-Me-Right creates awareness about the APPR, enhances communication with accurate information, simplifies finding and understanding it, and clarifies compensation eligibility.

For the Client

  • Streamlined communication with passengers
  • Greater transparency and satisfaction
  • Ready to use awareness materials

For the Users

  • Accessible information about their rights
  • A simplified compensation process
  • Proactive awareness during disruptions
Success Metrics

Track visitors, site duration, and compensation checks. Gather feedback on usefulness and clarity. Measure distribution and visibility of promotional materials. Monitor changes in compensation claims submitted.

11

Limitations

Real Time Data

Delays in real time flight data could affect compensation assessments.

Internet Connectivity

Passengers might struggle to use the site with poor internet connectivity.

Accurate Input

The eligibility checker relies on accurate input, and errors could cause wrong assessments.

Scenario Coverage

The site may not cover every scenario, requiring further clarification from airlines.

12

Scalability

Regional expansion with local adaptations for each market.

Partner with the travel industry to extend reach.

Integrate the website into different travel platforms.

13

Reflection

The regulation already existed. What passengers lacked was a way to understand it in the moment they actually needed it.

This project focused on translating the APPR into clear guidance at the exact point of disruption, without touching airline systems or policy itself.

The biggest takeaway is that accessibility starts as a cognitive problem before it becomes a visual one. Clear structure, transparent reasoning, and the right timing reduce confusion far more effectively than simply adding more information ever could.
