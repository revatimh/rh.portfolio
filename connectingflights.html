Connecting Flights

Understanding Passenger Rights and Flight Disruption Experiences

My Role

Research Lead
Qualitative and quantitative data collection, thematic analysis, insight synthesis
Design Lead
System map, wireframes, website design

The Problem

Most passengers are not aware of their rights as a passenger, especially during a flight disruption. The Air Passenger Protection Regulations exist, but people either have never read the regulations, or don't know what applies to their specific situation. Hence, there is a disconnect between what they expect, and what they recieve.

This creates a gap between what airlines are required to provide and what passengers think they can claim.

Research Question

What are the factors contributing to the communication gap between the services provided to passengers and their expectations during disrupted connecting flights, particularly concerning passenger rights and compensation?

Research Approach

This research followed a mixed-methods approach over 12 weeks.

Surveys were used to collect the quantitative data. It helped in understanding common patterns, such as how often people missed connections, how aware they were of compensation rules, and where confusion was most common.

Interviews used to collect qualitative data. It helped in understanding people’s thinking during disruptions, including how they decided what to do next and why they felt unsure or frustrated.

Using both together made it easier to see the bigger picture while also understanding the reasons behind people’s choices.

Key Findings

1

Passengers are not aware of their rights

76.1% passengers hadn't read the Air Passenger Protection Regulations. Airlines mention it in terms and conditions, but there is no way to make sure passengers know what they're entitled to when things go wrong.

2

The document of the regulations is difficult to understand

People who tried to read the regulations described it as "reading a textbook." They wanted important points highlighted. The length and wording made it difficult to find the important information in the time of need.

3

People don't know if they qualify for compensation or not

44.8% participants had no idea when they were entitled for compensation. Some think any disruption means compensation, but there are specific criterias for each case. Even when people suspect they might qualify, they don't know how to claim it.

4

Limited communication breaks trust

Airlines can't always share full details about disruptions due to various limitations. Due to the insufficient communication about the disruptions and lack of context, passengers feel lack of trust towards the airlines.

Where to Focus

After learning about different factors involved, the focus was shifted on helping the passengers to be aware of their rights, their eligibility for compensation, and next steps to take when they face a disruption. They should get a clarity about the process.

Redesigning how airlines operate or rewriting the regulations was not necessary. The research discovered a gap and it was time to mend it.

The Solution

Project Inform-Me-Right

Project Inform-Me-Right is a website supported by airport awareness touchpoints. The goal is simple. Make passenger rights under APPR visible, understandable, and usable during flight disruptions.

01 AWARENESS

Awareness begins at the airport. QR codes are placed at gates, check-in counters, baggage claim areas, and seat pockets so passengers can access support immediately. Visual prompts and staff guidance direct people to the website during disruptions. The goal is to reach passengers at the exact moment confusion starts.

The intervention does not rely on passengers searching for rights themselves. It surfaces information in context. This shifts awareness from passive discovery to timely access.

02 ELIGIBILITY CHECKER

The Compensation Eligibility Checker helps passengers determine whether they qualify under APPR by entering flight details and disruption type. It walks users through structured inputs instead of forcing them to interpret legal text.

If eligible, it outlines next steps for filing a claim. If not, it explains why and references the relevant regulation. The focus is transparency. The outcome is not just yes or no. It is explained reasoning.

03 SIMPLIFIED APPR

The website presents APPR in structured, plain language without altering the original regulation. Complex policy text is supported with short explanations and organized by passenger situations rather than legal sections.

Content is broken into scannable sections to reduce cognitive load. The intent is comprehension under stress. It makes regulation readable without oversimplifying it.

Validation and Iteration

A mid-fidelity prototype of the eligibility checker was tested with participants who had experienced flight disruptions. The goal was to evaluate clarity, navigation, and understanding of eligibility logic.

Participants were able to determine their eligibility and explain the reasoning behind outcomes. Testing identified gaps in airport search, back navigation, and accessibility features.

What this showed: The testing reinforced that clarity and error recovery were more critical than feature expansion.

Reasons for the Solution

Information Gap

Passengers are often unaware of APPR or misunderstand eligibility criteria. The solution directly addresses this gap through structured awareness and guided explanation.

Policy Complexity

APPR language prioritizes legal precision over usability. The website reorganizes content around passenger needs without changing regulatory wording.

High Friction

Disruptions create stress and cognitive overload. Structured guidance reduces decision fatigue and prevents unnecessary confusion during high-pressure moments.

Operational Strain

Airline staff repeatedly answer eligibility questions. A self-service tool reduces repetitive explanations and redirects simple cases away from customer support channels.

What This Does

For Passengers

  • Check eligibility for compensation independently, without waiting on hold or going through dense policy documents.
  • Understand why they qualify or do not qualify through structured explanations tied to regulation.
  • Receive next steps for submitting claims instead of guessing or submitting incomplete requests.

For Airlines

  • Decrease repetitive eligibility inquiries that consume customer service resources during disruptions.
  • Lower volume of claims submitted by passengers who do not meet compensation criteria.
  • Provide consistent eligibility explanations aligned with APPR regulation, reducing perceived unfairness or misinformation.
HOW TO MEASURE SUCCESS
How many people complete the eligibility checker How long they spend reading the simplified rights Whether customer service calls about eligibility actually go down Ratio of approved to denied claims Satisfaction scores after disruptions

Limitations

Manual Input

The checker depends on passenger-entered flight details. Incorrect or incomplete inputs may affect eligibility outcomes and reduce perceived accuracy.

No Live Data

The prototype does not integrate real-time flight status. Eligibility assessments rely on static inputs rather than verified operational data.

Edge Cases

Complex travel scenarios such as multi-airline itineraries may require manual review beyond structured rule logic.

Internet Access

The website requires stable internet access, which may not always be available during travel disruptions.

Next Steps

Better Testing

  • Test with older passengers and varied travel classes to validate usability across groups.
  • Measure which airport touchpoints drive meaningful traffic to the website.
  • Assess whether understanding eligibility changes actual claim submission behavior.

Better Implementation

  • Refine eligibility logic using anonymized airline compensation records.
  • Integrate real-time disruption data to reduce manual entry errors.
  • Launch with one airline or airport to measure operational impact.

Scalability

  • Adapt content for international travelers using clear translation frameworks.
  • Modify logic to align with different regional passenger rights regulations.
  • Ensure strong performance across devices for real-time airport access.

Reflection

The core issue was not the absence of regulation. It was comprehension. APPR exists, but it is structured for compliance, not for passengers navigating uncertainty in real time.

This project focused on translating regulation into structured guidance at the moment of disruption. It does not attempt to redesign airline systems or policy frameworks.

The strongest takeaway is that accessibility is cognitive before it is visual. Clear structure, transparent logic, and contextual timing reduce confusion more effectively than adding more information ever will.
